# Chalk Talk — Basketball Concepts & Coaching Language

Research notes for the voice-to-whiteboard app. Every term below has:

- **Coach**: a one-line definition the way a coach would say it on the bench
- **Kid**: a phrase that works for 8–12 year olds
- **Heard as**: spoken synonyms plus likely speech-recognizer mishearings, to use as aliases

Players are always O1–O5 (offense) and X1–X5 (defense), never real names. Court spots used here match `SPOTS` in `src/sports/basketball.js`.

Sources are numbered `[n]` and listed at the bottom. Where sources disagree, the most common modern meaning is used and the difference is noted.

## 1. Spacing & spots

| Term | Coach | Kid | Heard as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Top / point** | The spot straight out from the rim, at the top of the key above the 3-point line | "The top, right in the middle" | top of the key, point, top of key, the key |
| **Slot** | Where the lane line, if you extended it, meets the 3-point line, between the top and the wing [2] | "Halfway between the top and the wing" | slots, right slot, left slot, guard spot, "the lot", "slaw" |
| **Wing** | The 3-point line at about free-throw-line height, on the side | "The side, even with the free-throw line" | wings, right wing, left wing, "wink", "ring" |
| **45 / 45 spot** | Wing-to-corner area at a 45° angle to the rim; also the angle a corner player cuts on [2] | "Halfway between the wing and the corner" | forty-five, four five, the 45, "for tea five" |
| **Corner** | Where the 3-point line meets the baseline, the shortest three | "Stand in the corner, feet behind the line" | corners, right corner, deep corner, "coroner" |
| **Short corner** | Baseline area between the lane and the corner, inside the arc [2] | "Halfway to the corner, close to the basket" | short corner, "sport corner", baseline short |
| **Dunker spot** | Just outside the lane on the baseline, next to the rim, where a big waits for a dump-off [2] | "Wait right by the rim on the baseline" | dunker, dunkers spot, dunk spot, "donker", "drunk her spot" |
| **Block / low post** | The marks on the lane line closest to the rim | "The first box on the side of the paint" | blocks, low block, low post, "the lock" |
| **Elbow** | Where the free-throw line meets the lane line | "The corner of the free-throw line" | elbows, high post corner, "el bow", "elbo" |
| **High post / free-throw line** | The middle of the free-throw line area | "Middle of the free-throw line" | high post, foul line, FT line, "free throw" |
| **Nail** | The middle of the free-throw line, the classic help spot for a defender one pass away [2] | "The spot in the middle where a helper waits" | the nail, "nale", "knell" |
| **Paint / lane** | The rectangle under the basket | "The painted box" | paint, lane, key, "the pain" |
| **Lift** | An off-ball player moves up (e.g. corner to wing) to give the driver room and a passing angle | "Move up so they can see you" | lift up, lift it, "left" |
| **Drift** | When the ball drives baseline, the weak-side wing slides down to the corner [2] | "Slide down to the corner" | drift down, drift to the corner, "drip", "trift" |
| **Fill / fill behind** | Replace the spot a driver or cutter just left [2][9] | "Fill the empty spot" | fill, fill behind, fill the spot, "feel", "phil" |
| **Spacing** | Stay 12–15 ft apart so one defender can't guard two | "Two big steps apart, like stars in the sky" | space, spread out, spread the floor, "facing" |
| **5-out** | All five players on the perimeter, nobody posted inside [9] | "Everybody around the 3-point line" | five out, 5 out, "five down", "fight out" |
| **4-out-1-in** | Four on the perimeter, one big in the post [9] | "Four outside, one big by the basket" | four out one in, 4 out 1 in, "for out won in" |
| **Ball side / help side** | The half of the floor the ball is on / the other half | "Ball side and helper side" | strong side, weak side, help side, helpside |
| **Empty side** | A side of the floor with no offensive player, to give a ball screen or post room | "Leave that side empty" | clear out, cleared side, "empty sight" |

## 2. Off-ball actions (cuts and screens away from the ball)

| Term | Coach | Kid | Heard as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Basket cut** | Cut straight to the rim after passing (pass and cut) [9] | "Pass and go to the rim" | basket cut, cut to the basket, "bask it cut" |
| **Give-and-go** | Pass, cut, get it right back | "Give it, then go get it back" | give and go, give n go, "give an' go" |
| **Backdoor** | When the defender denies the pass, cut behind them to the rim | "If they're in the way, go behind them" | back door, back-door, back cut, "black door" |
| **V-cut** | Walk the defender in, then plant and pop out to get open | "Go in, then pop out like a V" | v cut, vee cut, "be cut", "we cut" |
| **L-cut** | Walk up the lane, then cut sharply out to the wing | "Walk up, then step out like an L" | l cut, el cut, "elk cut" |
| **45 cut** | Corner player cuts toward the rim at an angle when their defender helps on a drive [2] | "If your guy leaves, slide in toward the rim" | forty-five cut, 45 cut, slash |
| **Flash** | Weak-side player cuts hard into the lane/high post to show for the ball | "Run into the middle, hands up" | flash, flash to the middle, flash middle, "flesh" |
| **Down screen / pin-down** | Screener sets a screen facing the baseline so a teammate can come up to the ball [6] | "Screen down so your teammate can come up" | pin down, pindown, down screen, "pin town" |
| **Back screen** | Screen set behind a defender (screener's back to the rim) so a teammate cuts to the basket | "Sneak behind their defender and stand still" | back screen, backscreen, "bad screen" |
| **Flare screen** | Screen that lets a shooter fade away from the ball for a shot | "Fade away for a shot" | flare, flare screen, "flair", "player screen" |
| **Cross screen** | Screen across the lane, usually block to block, to free a post | "Screen across the paint" | cross screen, across screen, "cross scream" |
| **UCLA cut** | Passer cuts to the rim off a back screen from the high post (Wooden's high-post offense) [12] | "Pass to the wing, then cut off the big's back" | UCLA, U C L A, UCLA screen, "you see LA" |
| **Flex** | Baseline (flex) screen for a cutter across the lane, then screen-the-screener down screen [6][13] | "Cut across the bottom, then screen for the screener" | flex cut, flex screen, flex offense, "flecks" |
| **Screen the screener (STS)** | After setting a screen, the screener gets screened right away | "The screener gets a screen too" | screen the screener, STS, "screen to screener" |
| **Stagger** | Two screens in a row on the same side for one cutter | "Two screens in a line, run off both" | staggered, stagger screen, double stagger, "swagger" |
| **Floppy** | Shooter starts under the rim with a single screen on one side and a stagger/double on the other; chooses one | "Start under the rim, pick a side" | floppy, flop, "sloppy", "floppy set" |
| **Zipper** | Player at the block cuts straight up to the top off a down screen [12] | "Zip straight up to the top like a zipper" | zipper, zipper cut, zip, "zip her" |
| **Ram** | Off-ball screen *for the ball screener*, right before they set a ball screen (screener gets screened) | "Screen the screener, then it's a pick and roll" | ram, ram screen, "ran", "lamb" |
| **Iverson cut** | Wing cuts across the floor above two elbow screeners (horns) to catch at the other wing | "Run across the top past both bigs" | Iverson, Iverson cut, "I-version", "eye verse in" |
| **Chicago** | Pin-down that flows straight into a dribble handoff (DHO) [5][6] | "Come off a screen, then grab a handoff" | Chicago, Chicago action, Chi-town, "she cargo" |
| **Zoom** | Same idea as Chicago (pin-down into DHO); many teams use "Zoom" when it starts from the elbow/block [5][7] | "Zoom up and grab it from the big" | zoom, zoom action, elbow zoom, "boom", "zoo" |
| **Ghost screen** | Screener runs at the ball like a screen but never makes contact, then pops or slips [12] | "Pretend to screen, then pop away" | ghost, ghost screen, "goes screen", "post screen" |
| **Slip** | Screener cuts to the rim before contact because the defense jumps the screen [12] | "Before they bump you, sneak to the rim" | slip, slip screen, slip it, "flip" |
| **Shake** | When a teammate drives, the player near them moves to a new open spot (often baseline) | "Your teammate drives, you shake to a new spot" | shake, shake up, "shape" |
| **Relocate** | After passing (or when a teammate drives), move to an open spot behind the defense's eyes | "Pass and move to a new spot" | relocate, re-locate, "real low kate" |

## 3. On-ball actions

| Term | Coach | Kid | Heard as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pick-and-roll (PnR)** | Ball screen, screener rolls to the rim | "Use the wall, the wall rolls to the rim" | pick and roll, PNR, P N R, ball screen, "pick in roll" |
| **Pick-and-pop** | Ball screen, screener steps out for a shot | "Use the wall, the wall pops out to shoot" | pick and pop, pop, "pick and pot" |
| **Drag screen** | Ball screen in transition, before the defense is set [5] | "Screen while we're still running" | drag, drag screen, double drag, "drug screen" |
| **Double drag / "77"** | Two trailers set back-to-back drag screens in transition; one rolls, one pops | "Two screens in a row on the run" | double drag, 77, seventy-seven, "seven seven" |
| **Spain PnR** | Top pick-and-roll where a third player back-screens the roller's defender, then pops [3][8] | "A pick and roll where someone screens the big's defender" | Spain, Spain pick and roll, stack PnR, "spane", "pain" |
| **Horns** | 1-2-2 set: bigs on both elbows, shooters in the corners; ball handler can use either ball screen [4] | "Two bigs on the elbows, like bull horns" | horns, horn set, horn, "corns", "harness" |
| **Horns twist** | Two consecutive ball screens from the two elbow bigs; handler changes direction [4] | "Go one way, then turn back off the second screen" | horns twist, twist, "horns quist" |
| **Horns flare** | From horns, one big sets a flare screen for a shooter while the other ball-screens [3][4] | "One big screens for the shooter" | horns flare, flare out of horns |
| **Delay / elbow** | Big catches at the top/elbow and becomes the passer; guards cut backdoor or come get a handoff [5] | "Pass to the big, then cut or come get it" | delay, elbow, big at the elbow, "the lay", "dee lay" |
| **DHO / "get"** | Dribble handoff: dribbler hands the ball to a teammate running past, like a screen with the ball | "Hand it, don't throw it" | DHO, D H O, dribble handoff, handoff, hand off, get, "the H O" |
| **Pistol / "21"** | Early offense: point guard and wing (1 and 2) play a quick give-and-go/handoff on one side, then a big screens [10] | "Push it, pass to the wing, chase for it back" | pistol, 21, twenty one, two one, "pisstol" |
| **Rip** | A UCLA-style back screen / cut off the high post; also used for a quick cut past the high post | "Rip past the big to the rim" | rip, rip cut, rip screen, "trip" |
| **Step-up screen** | Ball screen set on the side, stepping up from below the ball (often early offense) | "Big steps up to screen from below" | step up, step-up screen, "set up" |
| **Empty / side PnR** | Pick-and-roll on a side with no teammate in the corner | "Pick and roll with lots of room" | empty corner, side pick and roll |
| **Post entry** | Pass into a post player who seals the defender | "Throw a bounce pass to the big who's holding his spot" | post entry, feed the post, throw it in, "post pantry" |
| **Seal** | Post player pins the defender behind them with body/arm and shows a target hand | "Sit on him and show your hand" | seal, seal it, "steal" |
| **Triple threat** | Catch facing the rim, ready to shoot, pass, or dribble | "Shoot, pass, or dribble: ready for all three" | triple threat, triple-threat, "triple thread" |
| **Jump stop** | Land on two feet at once so you can use either foot as a pivot | "Land on two feet, boom" | jump stop, two-foot stop, "jumps top" |

| Term | Coach | Kid | Heard as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Three-lane break** | Ball in the middle, two runners wide, rim to rim | "Ball in the middle, run wide" | three lanes, 3-lane, fast break, "free lane" |
| **Rim run** | First big sprints straight to the rim in transition | "Big sprints rim to rim" | rim run, rim runner, run the rim, "rim rum" |
| **Trailer** | The last player up the floor, often for a drag screen or a three | "Last one down, come behind the ball" | trailer, trail, "tailor" |

## 5. Defense

| Term | Coach | Kid | Heard as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Man-to-man** | Each defender guards one player | "You have one player. Stay with them." | man to man, man, player-to-player, "man and man" |
| **Help / gap / nail** | Off-ball defenders sag toward the ball in the gaps and the nail, ready to stop a drive [2] | "One step toward the ball, ready to help" | help defense, help side, gap, sink, "hell defense" |
| **See ball, see man** | Off-ball stance with vision on both the ball and your player | "Point at the ball and your player" | ball you man, pistols, see both, "sea ball" |
| **Jump to the ball** | On every pass, all defenders move toward where the ball went [14] | "When the ball moves, you move" | jump to the ball, jump to ball, "jump the ball" |
| **Deny** | One-pass-away defender takes away the passing lane | "Hand in the passing lane" | deny, denial, one pass away, "the night" |
| **Closeout** | Run at a shooter, then chop your steps with hands high [14] | "Run, then short steps, hands up" | close out, closeout, "clothes out" |
| **Tag** | Weak-side defender bumps the roller to slow them, then recovers | "Bump the roller, then get back" | tag, tag the roller, "tack" |
| **X-out** | Rotation where two weak-side defenders swap to cover a skip pass | "Make an X: you take mine, I take yours" | x out, ex out, "eggs out" |
| **Rotate** | When a teammate helps, the next defender covers for them | "Help the helper" | rotate, rotation, help the helper |
| **ICE / "down" / "blue"** | Vs a side ball screen, on-ball defender jumps to the top and forces the dribbler to the sideline/baseline; big sits low [8] | "Block the middle, push them to the sideline" | ice, ice it, down, blue, push, "eyes", "ice it down" |
| **Drop** | Screener's defender sags back to the paint to protect the rim [8] | "Big stays back by the rim" | drop, drop coverage, sag, "drip" |
| **Hedge / show** | Screener's defender jumps out in front of the dribbler briefly, then recovers [8] | "Jump out to slow them, then run back" | hedge, show, hard hedge, "edge", "head" |
| **Switch** | Defenders swap players on a screen [8] | "Switch! You take mine, I take yours" | switch, switch it, "swish" |
| **Blitz / trap** | Both defenders trap the ball handler off the screen [8] | "Two of us trap the ball" | blitz, trap, double, "blitzed" |
| **Over / under** | Fight over the top of a screen vs. go under it | "Over the top" / "Go under the wall" | over, under, go over, go under |
| **Box out / "hit and get"** | On the shot, find a player, make contact (hit), then go get the ball [14] | "Find, hit, get it" | box out, boxout, block out, hit and get, "box doubt" |
| **2-3 zone** | Two defenders up top, three across the back, guarding areas not players | "Guard a space, not a player" | two three zone, 2 3, zone, "too three" |
| **Full-court press** | Defend the inbound and the whole floor, often trapping | "Guard them all the way" | press, full court press, "dress" |

## 7. What's age-appropriate (8–12)

- **Man-to-man, no zones before 12.** The NBA & USA Basketball youth guidelines (2018) recommend player-to-player defense and discourage zone defense for younger age groups (no zones until 12 and up), to build individual defensive skills and keep kids moving [1][1a]. FIBA youth/mini-basketball guidance is stricter, with no zones until about 14 [1b]. Zone *offense* (attacking a zone) is introduced around 12–14 [1b].
- **Age bands.** The guidelines use 7–8, 9–11, 12–14 and high school, with smaller balls, lower rims and shorter games for the youngest groups [1][1a].
- **5-out pass-cut-fill as the base offense.** Breakthrough Basketball and Hoop Coach both recommend a simple 5-out "pass, basket cut, fill the open spot" motion for youth teams, kept until it's automatic before adding screens away or ball screens [9][9a]. Breakthrough's own coaches warn that many teams add options too soon [9b].
- **Screens come after cutting.** Teach in order: spacing → pass & cut → fill → screen away (down/back screens) → ball screens and DHOs. Named NBA actions (Spain, Chicago, Horns, Pistol) suit roughly 11–12+ or travel teams and are good *concept demos* for younger kids ("two screens in a row", "pretend screen").
- **Defense language.** For 8–12, keep it to stance, "ball, you, basket" (see ball, see man), jump to the ball, closeouts and box out [14]. Coverage names (ICE/drop/hedge) are for 12+; the drawings still work for coach education.
- **Presses.** Many youth leagues limit or ban full-court pressing under 10–12 (check local rules). Teaching a 1-4 / "4 across" press break is still useful because the ideas (flash to the ball, middle, then long) are just spacing and cutting [11].

## 8. Design notes for the speech layer

- Coaches say numbers as words and digits: "21" / "twenty-one" / "two one"; "77" / "seventy-seven"; "5 out" / "five out". Normalize number words before matching.
- Acronyms are spelled out ("D H O", "P N R", "U C L A", "B L O B", "S L O B"). Join single letters before matching.
- Watch short, risky aliases: "get", "down", "show", "push", "box", "stack", "seal", "chase", "drop" are everyday words. Only accept them when the utterance also has a play cue ("run a…", "show me…", "draw…") or in a defense context.
- Common mishearings: horns→"corns/harness", Spain→"spane/pain", ghost→"goes/post", slip→"flip", flex→"flecks", zoom→"boom/zoo", ICE→"eyes", hedge→"edge/head", drift→"drip", dunker→"donker".
